PROMETHEUS LEARNING AND INTERVIEW GUIDE
Basic -> Intermediate -> Advanced -> Expert
==========================================

GOAL
----
Prometheus collects and stores time-series metrics, usually by scraping
HTTP endpoints. Learn metric types, labels, scrape configuration, PromQL,
alerts, service discovery, cardinality, retention, and failure modes.

LEARNING PATH
-------------
Basic:
  - Time series, timestamp, metric name, labels, scrape target.
  - Counter, gauge, histogram, summary; Prometheus exposition format.
Intermediate:
  - PromQL selectors, range vectors, rate/increase, aggregation,
    recording rules, alerting rules, exporters, and service discovery.
Advanced:
  - Cardinality control, remote write/read, federation, HA, retention,
    relabeling, scrape health, and alert noise reduction.
Expert:
  - SLO-based alerting, capacity planning, multi-tenant governance,
    resilient metric pipelines, and query performance.

CORE PROMQL EXAMPLES
--------------------
    # Select a metric series with a label filter.
    http_requests_total{job="api", status="500"}
    # Calculate per-second request rate over five minutes, by service.
    sum by (service) (rate(http_requests_total[5m]))
    # Calculate the fraction of requests taking under 0.3 seconds.
    sum(rate(http_request_duration_seconds_bucket{le="0.3"}[5m]))
      / sum(rate(http_request_duration_seconds_count[5m]))

REAL-WORLD INCIDENT: PROMETHEUS MEMORY GROWS RAPIDLY
----------------------------------------------------
Inspect active series and label cardinality. Search for labels containing
request IDs, user IDs, full URLs, or other unbounded values. Remove or
normalize the high-cardinality label at instrumentation/relabeling, then
review retention and storage sizing. Do not solve an unbounded series
problem only by increasing memory.

REAL-WORLD INCIDENT: TARGET IS DOWN / METRICS MISSING
----------------------------------------------------
Check Prometheus target health and scrape error, then validate DNS,
network path, endpoint path/port, TLS/auth, and service discovery labels.
Confirm the app exposes metrics and that relabeling did not drop them.
Separate exporter failure from Prometheus scrape failure.

50 INTERVIEW QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
----------------------------------
01. Q: What is Prometheus? A: An open-source monitoring system that scrapes and stores labeled time-series data and evaluates queries/rules.
02. Q: What is a time series? A: Samples of one metric identified by its metric name and label set over time.
03. Q: What is a label? A: A key/value dimension that identifies a series; keep its possible values bounded.
04. Q: What is a counter? A: A monotonically increasing value that can reset, such as total requests.
05. Q: What is a gauge? A: A value that can rise or fall, such as current queue depth.
06. Q: What is a histogram? A: Observations accumulated into buckets plus count and sum, enabling distribution estimates.
07. Q: What is a summary? A: A client-side distribution summary with quantiles that generally cannot be aggregated across instances like histogram buckets.
08. Q: What is a scrape? A: Prometheus periodically fetches metric samples from a configured target.
09. Q: What is an exporter? A: A service that exposes metrics for a system that does not natively provide Prometheus metrics.
10. Q: What is a target? A: An endpoint Prometheus attempts to scrape.
11. Q: What does `up` mean? A: A built-in series indicating whether the last scrape of a target succeeded.
12. Q: What is PromQL? A: Prometheus's expression language for selecting, transforming, and aggregating time-series data.
13. Q: Instant vector versus range vector? A: Instant vectors hold latest samples per series; range vectors contain samples over a time interval.
14. Q: What does `rate()` do? A: Estimates per-second counter increase over a range and adjusts for resets.
15. Q: `rate()` versus `increase()`? A: `rate` returns a per-second rate; `increase` estimates total increase over the selected interval.
16. Q: Why apply rate before aggregation? A: It handles counter resets per series before combining results.
17. Q: What is a recording rule? A: A scheduled PromQL expression whose result is stored as a new series for reuse or speed.
18. Q: What is an alerting rule? A: A condition evaluated over time that creates alert state and may be sent to Alertmanager.
19. Q: What is Alertmanager? A: A component that groups, routes, silences, and deduplicates alerts.
20. Q: What is an alert `for` duration? A: A period a condition must remain true before firing, reducing transient noise.
21. Q: What is cardinality? A: The number of distinct label combinations/series; high cardinality increases memory and query cost.
22. Q: Why avoid user IDs in labels? A: They create an unbounded number of series and can expose sensitive identifiers.
23. Q: How do you measure request latency percentiles? A: Use histogram buckets and `histogram_quantile` over correctly aggregated bucket rates.
24. Q: How do you calculate error ratio? A: Divide the rate of error requests by total request rate, guarding for zero traffic.
25. Q: What is service discovery? A: Dynamic target discovery from sources such as Kubernetes, cloud APIs, or static configuration.
26. Q: What is relabeling? A: A rule stage that changes, keeps, or drops target/sample labels and targets.
27. Q: Why can a target be missing? A: Discovery, relabeling, DNS, access, endpoint, or configuration errors can remove or block it.
28. Q: What does `scrape_interval` control? A: How often Prometheus collects samples from a target.
29. Q: What is scrape timeout? A: The maximum allowed duration for one scrape; it should not exceed the scrape interval.
30. Q: What is a stale series? A: A series no longer reported or marked stale, so instant queries stop treating it as current.
31. Q: What is remote write? A: Shipping scraped samples to a compatible remote storage or metrics service.
32. Q: What is federation? A: Scraping selected aggregated series from another Prometheus server.
33. Q: What does retention control? A: How long local time-series data is kept, subject to storage and operational limits.
34. Q: How do you reduce PromQL cost? A: Narrow selectors/time ranges, use recording rules for repeated expensive expressions, and control cardinality.
35. Q: Why are missing metrics not always zero? A: The series may not have been created, scraped, or retained; use explicit absence handling.
36. Q: What is an SLI? A: A measured indicator of user-visible service behavior, such as successful request ratio.
37. Q: What is an SLO? A: A target over time for an SLI, such as a defined availability objective.
38. Q: What is an error budget? A: The tolerated unreliability implied by an SLO over its measurement window.
39. Q: How should alerts be designed? A: Alert on actionable user impact or imminent risk, with clear ownership and runbook context.
40. Q: Why can a histogram percentile be misleading? A: Bucket resolution, low traffic, aggregation mistakes, or missing buckets can distort estimates.
41. Q: What is Prometheus HA? A: Running independent replicas to reduce monitoring gaps; deduplication may be provided by a compatible query layer.
42. Q: Does Prometheus automatically deduplicate HA replicas? A: Not as a universal distributed-storage feature; deployments commonly use an external compatible layer.
43. Q: How do you protect the metrics endpoint? A: Restrict network access and apply supported TLS/auth at the application or proxy.
44. Q: What is a black-box probe? A: An external check that tests a service from a client-like perspective, often using the blackbox exporter.
45. Q: Exporter is up but metric is absent. What do you check? A: Exporter output, metric name, scrape labels, metric relabeling, and whether the metric has been emitted.
46. Q: Why can `rate` return no data? A: Too few samples, wrong metric name/range, scrape gaps, or counter not yet observed.
47. Q: What is a counter reset? A: A counter returning to a lower value, often after process restart; rate functions account for resets.
48. Q: How do you monitor Prometheus itself? A: Scrape its own operational metrics and alert on target, rule-evaluation, storage, and resource health.
49. Q: What is a useful first incident query? A: `up` for scrape state, then relevant application rates, errors, latency, and saturation.
50. Q: Describe a good metrics design. A: Instrument user outcomes with bounded labels, meaningful types, actionable queries, and tested alert/runbook behavior.
